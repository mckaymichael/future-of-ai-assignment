The Future of AI in Music

A scenario analysis exploring how Artificial Intelligence could reshape the music industry over the next decade.

2027
2027

The AI Flood: Playlists Fill Up, Trust Starts to Fracture

Streaming platforms are flooded with AI-generated tracks and synthetic “artists,” some landing real label deals while human musicians struggle for visibility. We are already seeing fully AI-generated personas with AI voices and beats securing massive record deals while real talent is sidelined.

By 2027, the most realistic scenario is oversaturation. Generative tools make it trivial to upload thousands of tracks, leading to a brutal landscape for discovery. While this is emotionally demotivating for human creators, it acts as a painful correction where audiences slowly begin to value authenticity again.

"It's highly demotivating to spend three years crafting an album only to hear AI instantly produce something passable... it is a temporary kick in the teeth."
— Ryan, Audio Professional

Vancouver nightlife is taking a hit, and AI-assisted music demotivates artists

Faced with an endless deluge of passable AI-generated content, emerging artists lose the drive to build careers, significantly impacting local, grassroots music scenes.

A solo artist in a developing country wins the first-ever Grammy using AI

AI gives previously under-resourced creators the chance to compete on the global stage. However, human ingenuity has often achieved this boundary-pushing genre fusion without AI already.

2031
2031

Quarantined AI: Human-Only Spaces Become a Selling Point

By 2031, AI music has its own major platforms and clearly labelled sections on streaming apps, while human-only spaces become a premium experience. Listeners, exhausted by years of AI spam, demand transparency.

Major services introduce AI labels, filters, and separate tabs for synthetic content. Over time, indie platforms like Bandcamp double down on human-only catalogues as a brand promise, protecting human scenes and artists.

"I want AI to do my taxes so I can do this stuff. Not AI to do this stuff, so I can do my taxes."
— Ryan, Audio Professional

AI takes over electronic music, forcing artists into other popular genres

While AI improves at generating electronic styles, top-tier electronic artists (like Deadmau5 or Skrillex) will rely heavily on their unique live setups to prevent AI from fully taking over their specific scenes.

Music-producing jobs are plummeting due to AI

The trend of mass job replacement in production stumbles as the massive energy and processing power required for AI to recycle heavy arrangements proves unsustainable compared to human efficiency.

2036
2036

Dead Artists on Tour: When Labels Don’t Tell You They’re Gone

In the darkest 2036 scenario, labels quietly continue releasing “new” music and even live-streamed performances from artists who died years earlier, leveraging voice-printing and performance synthesis without disclosure.

The technology to clone voices becomes mature and cheap. With obvious economic incentives to maintain infinite releases from catalogue-rich artists, the ethical lines of disclosure and consent are routinely ignored.

"Creating printed voices to hide an artist’s death is freaky... but it is actually going to definitely happen someday."
— Ryan, Audio Professional

The bell curve is flattening; any artist can improve their chances of success

While AI-integrated workflows boost the baseline quality of music, audiences grow deeply skeptical. Fans lose trust in artists who secretly use AI because music remains fundamentally about capturing a singular human moment.

AI gives small artists better resources and increases the chances of making it big

Predictive generation allows solo creators to afford massive productions, instantly generating realistic session musicians, like an 8-string heavy metal guitarist, to realize ideas they couldn't otherwise afford.

Will AI Replace the Artist or Elevate the Master?

As generative models become ubiquitous, the ultimate scarcity won't be high-quality music—it will be human authenticity. Are we building tools to eliminate technical friction, or are we accidentally replacing the very soul of the creative process?
